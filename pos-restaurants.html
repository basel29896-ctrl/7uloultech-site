Restaurant & lounge POS

The floor, the kitchen
and the bar, in one system.

7uloultech builds and deploys restaurant POS systems in Jordan. The product is CashierPOS: visual multi-floor table plans, kitchen and drinks display screens, shisha queue management, entrance ticketing, configurable service charge, and LAN sync across terminals. Each floor carries its own VAT rate and Article-5 seller identity on receipts.

It is offline-first, so service continues through an internet outage, and fully bilingual Arabic and English with RTL layouts. Staff permissions are enforced on the server, not merely hidden in the interface. 7uloultech supplies the hardware and installs on site across Jordan or remotely.

Built around how a venue actually works

A restaurant is several businesses sharing a roof: a floor that seats people, a kitchen that cooks, a bar that pours, and — in much of Amman — a shisha service with its own rhythm. CashierPOS treats each as a first-class part of the system rather than an add-on module.

Visual floor plans

Real table layouts with round, chair and wide shapes and configurable seat counts — the screen looks like the room.

Multi-floor, multi-tax

Every floor gets its own label, VAT rate and legal seller identity, printed correctly on that floor's receipts.

Kitchen & drinks displays

Orders route to the right screen the moment they're sent, so drinks don't queue behind food.

Shisha queue

Track what's lit, what's waiting and what needs a coal change — a workflow generic POS systems simply don't have.

Entrance ticketing

Charge at the door with a free-redemption category, so a ticket that includes a drink is handled by the system.

Service charge

A configurable percentage applied on top of tax, set per venue rather than hard-coded.

LAN multi-terminal

Several stations stay consistent across the venue, so every waiter sees the same floor state.

Server-enforced roles

Permissions are checked on the server. A waiter account can't reach revenue endpoints even directly.

Offline-first

The line drops and service continues. Orders sync when connectivity returns.

Getting live

  1. Intake. We record the venue in detail: every floor, its tables and seat counts, its VAT rate and legal seller identity, your menu structure, staff roles and permissions.
  2. Configuration. Your floors, menu, receipt footer and user accounts are built into your own copy of the system — not a shared tenant.
  3. Hardware. Terminals and thermal printers supplied and prepared before installation day.
  4. Installation. On site across Jordan or remote, including a staff walkthrough on the live system.
  5. Support. Monitoring, backups, a 12-hour maximum response time, and monthly change requests — two on the base tier, more on higher tiers.

Questions restaurant owners ask

Can different floors have different tax rates and seller details?

Yes. Each floor is configured independently with its own label, VAT rate and legal seller identity, and each floor's receipts carry that floor's Article-5 details. This suits venues where a rooftop and an indoor dining room are registered separately.

Is there a kitchen display system?

Yes — orders route to kitchen screens as soon as they're sent, with a separate drinks and bar display so beverages don't queue behind food.

Can it manage a shisha queue?

Yes. Shisha queue management is built in — what's lit, what's waiting, what needs a coal change. Most general restaurant POS systems have no concept of this at all.

What happens during an internet outage?

Service continues. CashierPOS is offline-first, taking and printing orders through the outage and synchronising when the connection returns.

Can I stop waiters from seeing revenue figures?

Yes, and properly. Each user's allowed views are enforced on the server rather than hidden in the interface, so a waiter account cannot reach reporting endpoints even by calling them directly. Reports access is granted explicitly to the people who need it.

Can several terminals run at once?

Yes. LAN sync keeps terminals consistent, so waiters on different stations see the same floor state.

How much does it cost and how long does it take?

Pricing is quoted per venue — it depends on floors, terminals, hardware and scope. There's no separate setup fee; setup, hosting, support and monthly change requests are included. Deployment typically runs four to eight weeks. Ask for a quote.
