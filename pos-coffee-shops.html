Café & coffee house POS

POS built for the
rhythm of a coffee bar.

7uloultech builds and deploys POS systems for coffee shops and cafés in Jordan. The product is CashierPOS: a counter-service point-of-sale that runs a rolling session instead of a table map, sends drinks straight to a bar display, tracks staff drink allowances, and keeps working when the internet drops. The interface is fully bilingual Arabic and English with RTL support, and receipts carry Article-5 seller identity with VAT extracted from gross prices.

Two Amman cafés run it in production today — GG Amman and The Dealer Cafe — both live for three months with no recorded downtime. 7uloultech supplies the hardware and installs on site or remotely.

Why a café is not a small restaurant

Most POS systems sold to cafés are restaurant systems with features hidden. That is the wrong shape. A coffee bar has no table turnover to manage, a queue that moves in seconds, and a drinks station that needs the order before the cashier has finished taking payment. CashierPOS is configured for that pattern rather than adapted to it.

Counter mode

A rolling single session — no table to assign, no floor plan to tap through. The barista rings the order and moves to the next customer.

Drinks display

Orders land on a dedicated bar screen the moment they're sent, so the drink is being made while payment completes.

Staff allowances

Free staff drinks are tracked by category and land in reporting, instead of becoming an argument at closing time.

Offline-first

The connection drops, the queue keeps moving. Orders are taken and printed, then synchronise when the line returns.

Arabic and English

A runtime toggle, not a translation layer. RTL-aware layouts, Arabic receipt labels, support in both languages.

Grows into seating

Add tables later and the same system switches to a visual floor plan with per-floor VAT. No migration, no second purchase.

How a deployment runs

  1. Intake. We sit with you and record the venue: store identity in Arabic and English, tax setup, menu categories, staff roles and who is allowed to see what.
  2. Configuration. Your menu, floors or counter setup, receipt footer and permissions are built into your own copy of the system.
  3. Hardware. We supply and prepare terminals and thermal printers so nothing needs sourcing on your side.
  4. Installation. On site in Amman or remotely, including staff walkthrough on the real system.
  5. Support. Monitoring, backups, a 12-hour maximum response time, and monthly change requests as the venue evolves.

Questions café owners ask

What's the best POS setup for a coffee shop with no table service?

A counter-service configuration. CashierPOS runs a rolling single session rather than a table map, so the barista rings each order straight through without assigning it to a table — which is how The Dealer Cafe runs. If you add seating later, the same system switches to a visual floor plan without being replaced.

Does it work if the internet goes down?

Yes. CashierPOS is offline-first: orders keep being taken and printed during a connectivity loss, then synchronise when the connection returns. In a café, a dropped line would otherwise stop the queue entirely.

Can it track free staff drinks?

Yes — tracked by category, so staff consumption is recorded against the right product group and shows up in reporting rather than being written off informally at closing.

Is it available in Arabic?

Yes. A full Arabic/English runtime toggle with RTL-aware layouts, Arabic receipt labels, and support in both languages. Arabic is a first-class interface, not an afterthought.

Do you supply the hardware?

Yes. Terminals, ESC/POS thermal receipt printers, and scanners where needed are supplied as part of the package. You don't source equipment or check compatibility yourself.

How much does it cost?

Pricing is quoted per venue, since it depends on terminal count, hardware and scope. There is no separate setup fee — setup is included in the subscription or purchase, along with hosting, support and monthly change requests. Ask for a quote and we'll price your venue specifically.

How long does it take to go live?

Typically four to eight weeks depending on scope, from first intake to a working floor. You see progress weekly rather than only at the end.
